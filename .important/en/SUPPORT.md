Standardoc is carried by a single person, on residual time, while
holding down two jobs on the side. Here's — honestly — how it helps,
and why.

---

## The material reality

[miralabs.tech](https://miralabs.tech) is my sole proprietorship — the
legal entity behind the `miralabs-tech` GitHub organization that hosts
Standardoc. The project **isn't a source of income**: I maintain it
because it's useful, not because it pays. Standardoc is the first
public piece of **StandarX**, the set of my OSS projects aiming to
standardize structural problems of the dev ecosystem.

I don't do promotion in general — for me the code speaks louder than
the marketing. Standardoc is the exception: it's the project that can
make concrete some ideas matured over years, so this is the first time
I'm making a real communication effort. The rest of the posture is
detailed in [notes](storytelling/notes.md).

## Free support

It counts for real, not out of politeness:

- **Star the repo** on
  [GitHub](https://github.com/miralabs-tech/standardoc) — the
  visibility of a solo infra project largely depends on it.
- **Open issues** — feedback, technical or global ideas, use cases.
  That's where the holes surface fastest.
- **Report bugs** with a reproducible case — it's gold for a solo
  maintainer.
- **Talk about it** — an article, a talk, a mention in your repo's
  README.

## OpenCollective

[StandarX on OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/standarx)
isn't a decorative button.

**What support concretely changes**: it lets me reduce the time spent
on subsistence jobs and move faster on Standardoc — and on the other
StandarX projects that follow. Without support, the shipping pace
depends on my residual free time, and my weeks are already busy
elsewhere. With support, the pace aligns with the priority the
community gives the project.

This isn't blackmail. It's a **material fact**. If the OpenCollective
doesn't take off, I'll keep shipping on my residual time — it'll just
be slower.

→ https://opencollective.com/standarx

## Working together

The sole-proprietorship status exists for a mundane reason: under
French law, to work independently (sponsoring, contracts,
collaborations), you have to be *something* in the eyes of the law.

**I'm open to indie opportunities via miralabs.tech — or to a classic
hire outright**, alongside the rest. If Standardoc (or the StandarX
trajectory) resonates with a need you have, [open a
discussion](https://github.com/miralabs-tech/standardoc/discussions) or
contact me via [miralabs.tech](https://miralabs.tech).

On the company side, sponsored work stays possible — coverage of an
off-roadmap language, integration into internal tooling, funded
prioritization of a feature. But the framing is clear: **nothing
sponsored ever closes the OSS Core surface**, and funding discussions
stay out of public discourse until 1.0 (cf.
[notes](storytelling/notes.md)).

## A paid companion tool, maybe one day

The focus stays on the open-source Core. **No SaaS, no subscription** —
as long as Standardoc has no server component that demands recurring
infra, there's no reason to charge a subscription, and none is
planned.

If a companion tool ever emerges (for example a post-1.0 local doc UI,
cf. [long-term vision](storytelling/vision-long-term.md)), three
things are locked in advance:

- **Local-only** — runs on your machine, no cloud, no SaaS dependency
- **Lifetime license, one-time purchase** — no subscription, no
  per-seat
- **Tooling scope** — never closes the OSS Core surface

Whether it ships or not depends on adoption and a clear demand. The
Core, for its part, stays locked: automatic conversion from
FSL-1.1-MIT to [plain MIT](../../LICENSE) on **April 26, 2028**,
whatever happens.
